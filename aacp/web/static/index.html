    How much of the gap to the oracle is closed?

    1 = as good as a per-instance oracle; 0 = no better than always running the single best solver (bnb); below 0 = worse than not selecting at all. 5-fold CV or leave-one-family-out, all features.

    Why accuracy is the wrong objective

    1 mscost of running bnb where greedy was enough
    vs
    50 scost of running greedy where it is not optimal

    A classifier counts both mistakes as "one error". On the fastest-to-optimum task, the accuracy-trained tree is right 77% of the time and is still worse than never selecting. The regret tree is right less often (74%) yet closes 66% of the gap; bagging it closes 99%.

    Honest caveat: on graph families the selector never saw in training, most selectors fall below the single best solver. See REPORT.md §5.5.

    Watch greedy walk into Johnson's trap.

    The bottom row L (k vertices) is an optimal cover. Each block Ri on top connects to groups of i vertices in L. Greedy always takes the highest-degree vertex, and the degrees are rigged so that is always an R vertex. It ends up with ≈ k·(H(k) − 1) vertices, the worst case of its O(log n) bound. Then add a little noise.

    Try k = 32, then move noise to 1%. The trap needs a knife-edge degree ordering, and a few random edges destroy it: the worst case occupies a vanishingly small part of instance space.
